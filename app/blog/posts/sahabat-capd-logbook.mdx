Sahabat CAPD is a monitoring application for people undergoing continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis (CAPD) and the clinicians who review their data. One of my main areas of work was the patient logbook: the workflow for recording a treatment exchange.

At first, it looked like a straightforward set of forms. In practice, it was a sequence of actions with timestamps, ordering, and conditions that determine what a patient can do next. Getting each revision right took much more than wiring fields to an API.

## Start with the logic, not the UI

I decided not to brute-force individual screens until they appeared to work. Instead, I treated the exchange flow as a logic problem first: identify the possible states, the valid transitions, and the data each step needed to carry forward.

That framing made it easier to reason about issues that kept returning during iteration, including exchange ordering, entry and exit times, redirects after an action, previous-date records, and error states. The flow was revised repeatedly as the product developed, rather than being treated as a one-time form implementation.

## Working within a shared deployment domain

The application was deployed beneath a shared ITS research domain at a project-specific path. That made navigation more complicated than a typical root-domain Next.js deployment.

I introduced a project-level router wrapper so application navigation could consistently account for the deployment path while keeping route calls readable in the feature code. The project later standardized the production configuration with Next.js base-path support and further routing refinements.

## Device-aware capture was part of the workflow

The logbook also included camera capture and image-detection integration. That meant working across device cameras and screen sizes, including camera-facing controls, capture guidance, previews, and follow-up fixes. It was a reminder that a browser feature can be technically available while still needing careful interface work to be dependable for real users.

## Outcome

Between March 2024 and May 2025, the deployed application recorded 415 completed treatment exchanges from 43 patients across 8 participating hospitals, with activity on 265 days. I also contributed health-indicator charts, medical-record editing, notifications, and guided help alongside the logbook work.

The project changed how I approach workflows with similar dependencies: I map the states and rules before implementing the interface.
